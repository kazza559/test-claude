## 1. Total stablecoin market cap: 1 Jan 2026, month-/quarter-ends, latest; YTD growth vs 2024/2025; ATHs and contractions

### Takeaway
Total stablecoin supply has been **flat for 2026**: $305.0B on 1 Jan 2026 and $305.2B on 21 Oct 2026, so **+0.04% YTD** (−0.2% vs 31 Dec 2025). Full-year growth was +56.5% in 2024 and +49.7% in 2025. Supply set a new all-time high of **$317.1B on 17 May 2026** (DL; other trackers put the peak at $320–323B). It then had its **first quarterly contraction since 2023**, falling 5.4% peak-to-trough to $299.9B on 11 Aug. It recovered to about $308–310B in late Sep/early Oct, then eased again. Supply has sat on a plateau of roughly $300–320B since late Oct 2025, which is about 12 months.

### Inferences
- The plateau is real and broad across data providers, but it is shallow: −5% peak-to-trough versus −35% in 2022–23. Pegs did not break. Redemptions concentrated in yield- and DeFi-linked coins (Section 2).
- Supply moved with the crypto cycle (Section 4: total crypto market cap fell about 52% from its Oct 2025 peak to its 1 Jul 2026 low), not with payments adoption. Payments metrics kept rising.
- The H1 2026 high in May came after a Feb–Apr rebuild. The Jun–Jul drawdown coincided with yield cuts (Ethena, Sky), the end of incentives (PyUSD/Kamino), Hyperliquid's USDC migration and risk-off macro (Fed hike risk, Section 7).

### Gaps
- No official or regulatory aggregate series exists, and tracker methodologies differ by $2–15B (for example, whether BUIDL or other tokenized funds are included). Monthly figures from other providers (Artemis, rwa.xyz, CoinGecko) were not pulled.
- One secondary report claims supply "dropped ~$15B on 2 Aug 2026" because of a "GENIUS Act yield ban". No such one-day move appears in DL, and the GENIUS Act is not yet effective, so I treat this claim as **unreliable**. — [search-indexed claim; not corroborated](https://www.allium.so/blog/bitcoin-com-cites-allium-data-in-the-15b-stablecoin-supply-drop-story/)

## 2. Supply by issuer in 2026: gainers, losers, market-share shifts

### Takeaway
**Both incumbents shrank in 2026**: USDT −2.3% YTD and USDC −4.1% YTD. Tether+Circle combined share fell from 86.1% to 83.6%, and was 89.2% on 1 Jan 2025. Supply outside the big two grew from $42.5B to $50.1B (13.9% → 16.4%).
- **Biggest relative gainers:** Paxos/GDN **USDG** (+149%), **RLUSD** (+88%), **USDD** (+110%), Sky **USDS** (+15%), **USD1** (+20%), BlackRock **BUIDL** (+47%), and euro coins (EURC +45%, EURCV +201%).
- **New 2026 entrants:** United Stables **U** ($1.58B), Anchorage/OSL **USDGO** ($1.28B), Open Standard **OUSD** ($0.83B in three weeks), Polymarket **pUSD** ($0.48B), **SoFiUSD** ($0.35B), Tether **USAT** ($0.18B).
- **Biggest losers:** Ethena **USDe** (−26% YTD; −68% from its Oct 2025 peak), **PYUSD** (−21%), **USDtb** (−39%), **FDUSD** (−41%), **USDai** (−60%) and **Resolv USR** (−98%, exploit).

## 5. Regulation in 2026

### Takeaway
**Implementation slowed in 2026, and the policy direction stayed favorable but unfinished.**
- **US federal:**
  - The GENIUS Act's one-year rulemaking deadline (18 Jul 2026) **was missed**.
  - OCC Comptroller Gould said on 15 Oct that the OCC aims to finalize its rule **by 31 Oct 2026**.
  - The Act's effective date is the earlier of **18 Jan 2027** or 120 days after final rules.
  - Treasury has made **no comparability determination** for any foreign regime, so USDT has no US status; Tether launched USAT instead.
  - **CLARITY Act:** the Tillis–Alsobrooks deal on stablecoin yield (1 May) and Senate Banking passage (May) were followed by a **failed cloture vote (49–50) on 15 Sep 2026** over Trump-crypto ethics. The bill is shelved into the midterms.
  - Circle won a final OCC trust charter (10 Jul).
- **US states:** California repealed its DFAL stablecoin chapter (30 Jun).
- **EU:** the MiCA transition ended 1 Jul 2026, and euro stablecoins roughly doubled YoY.
- **UK:** the BoE softened its regime (holding caps replaced by a £40B issuance cap).
- **Hong Kong:** licensed only HSBC and Anchorpoint (10 Apr).
- **Japan:** megabanks are targeting fiscal 2026.
- **South Korea:** the won-stablecoin law is still stalled.
- **Singapore:** consulted on a statutory regime (Sep–Oct).
- **Mainland China:** banned unapproved RMB stablecoins (6 Feb).

### Cited Findings

#### US: GENIUS Act implementation
- **Statutory clock:**
  - Regulators had to issue implementing rules by 18 Jul 2026.
  - The Act takes effect on the earlier of 18 Jan 2027 or 120 days after final rules.
  - Treasury, the OCC, FDIC and NCUA all missed the deadline; their packages remained proposals.
  - Comment periods were still open for the joint customer-identification (CIP) rule (to 21 Aug) and the FDIC AML proposal (to 4 Aug).
  - Sources: [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/408843/us-regulators-miss-genius-acts-one-year-deadline-for-final-stablecoin-rules); [Chapman tracker](https://www.chapman.com/publication-genius-act-rulemaking-tracker)
- **Rulemaking timeline:**
  - **Treasury ANPRM:** Sep 2025 (baseline).
  - **FDIC application NPRM:** Dec 2025, the first agency-specific rule.
  - **OCC NPRM:** 25 Feb 2026, a 367–376-page proposal creating 12 CFR Part 15. It covers licensing, reserves, capital, custody, reporting, fees and enforcement; comments were due 1 May 2026.
  - **FinCEN/OFAC AML and sanctions NPRM:** 10 Apr 2026; comments due 9 Jun 2026.
  - Sources: [Debevoise](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2026/03/occ-issues-comprehensive-genius-act-rulemaking-pro); [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/03/occ-unveils-proposed-rulemaking-to-implement-the-genius-act-a-new-era-for-u-s-s/); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/pt/insights/publications/2026/03/occ-proposes-comprehensive-rulemaking-to-implement-the-genius-act); [JD Supra (FinCEN/OFAC)](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/treasury-proposes-anti-money-laundering-2044497/)
- **Reported draft terms:** a $5M minimum capital floor for OCC-supervised issuers, and tiered liquidity with at least 10% of outstanding coins redeemable the same business day. **[reported]** — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/408843/us-regulators-miss-genius-acts-one-year-deadline-for-final-stablecoin-rules)
- **Yield anti-evasion:** the OCC proposal creates a rebuttable presumption that an issuer violates the GENIUS yield ban if it pays an affiliate or "related third party" that passes yield to holders. It carves out merchant discounts and white-label profit-sharing with non-affiliated partners. — [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/stablecoin-interest-yield-and-rewards-occ-proposes-sweeping-regulations-under); [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/crypto-policy-tracker/occ-proposed-rule-under-the-genius-act-six-key-takeaways)

- **Charters:**

### Inferences
- The US legal framework exists (GENIUS), but its rules are about a quarter late. The OCC final rule (targeted for 31 Oct) is the key near-term catalyst, because 120 days after final rules could pull the effective date to around late Feb/March 2027 only if all agencies finish. Otherwise the effective date stays 18 Jan 2027.
- The CLARITY failure leaves the **yield question unresolved**. Exchange and platform rewards (Coinbase USDC rewards, partner yield-sharing such as USDG and Open USD revenue sharing) continue under GENIUS and the OCC's anti-evasion proposal, without the CLARITY Section 404 ban. This supports challenger distribution models in the near term but adds legal uncertainty.
- Outside the US, regimes are tightening but working (MiCA, Hong Kong, the UK). Every new non-USD launch (HKDAP, JPYSC, Qivalis) is small next to USD coins, so non-USD supply is about 0.6% of the total.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether the OCC published its final rule before 21 Oct. As of 15 Oct the target was 31 Oct.
- I could not confirm the status of Treasury and Fed final rules, or of any state "substantially similar" certifications (due 18 Jul 2026).
- No confirmed 2026 Korean passage, UAE dirham-stablecoin approval or Hong Kong second batch.

## 6. TradFi / fintech / Big Tech entry during 2026 (late 2025 baseline); M&A confirmed vs rumored

### Takeaway
2026 was the year **incumbent payments and banking moved from pilots to owned infrastructure.**
- **Infrastructure and chains:** Mastercard agreed to buy **BVNK (up to $1.8B, 17 Mar)**. Stripe launched **Tempo mainnet (Mar)**. Circle launched **Arc mainnet (16 Sep)** with Visa, Mastercard, BlackRock, DTCC and ICE among validators. Robinhood launched its chain (1 Jul).

- **Settlement and launches:** Mastercard (3 Jun) and Visa (>$20B run rate) scaled stablecoin settlement. SoFi, Western Union and Tether's USAT launched coins.

## 10. What the evidence implies for the trend's fundamental momentum in 2026 (accelerating, steady or decelerating)

### Takeaway
**Supply has decelerated to a stall; usage and infrastructure are accelerating; regulation is moving but slower than expected.**
- **Balance sheet:** the float (about $305B, +0% YTD versus +50–57%/yr in 2024–25; the first quarterly contraction since 2023) has **clearly decelerated** and now tracks the crypto bear cycle and yield conditions.
- **Throughput:** record adjusted volumes, about 2x velocity, Visa settlement up more than 15x, CPN up 76% QoQ and payments up about 40–60% YoY are **accelerating**.
- **Institutional build-out** is **accelerating sharply**: Open USD, the 21-bank consortium, Mastercard–BVNK, Arc, Tempo, Robinhood Chain, HKDAP and SoFiUSD.
- **Regulation** is **steady to slightly decelerating**: GENIUS rules are late, CLARITY failed cloture, and Korea and Hong Kong are slow, while MiCA, the UK and Singapore advance.

On balance the "stablecoin trend" keeps **structural tailwinds**: distribution, rails and legal certainty all improved. But its **cyclical engine (crypto-native demand for collateral and yield, about 88% of demand per JPMorgan) is off**, so near-term supply growth depends on a crypto recovery or on new bank and fintech coins actually scaling in 2027.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Supply is decelerating; it is not collapsing.** Both majors held their pegs, redemptions were orderly and concentrated in yield products, and Tron's USDT and EM demand kept rising. The plateau looks like a 2026 rerun of the 2022–23 stall in mild form. That episode took about 19 months to bottom and about 31 months to regain its prior peak; this one fell only about 5%.
- **Quality of growth improved while quantity stalled.** Velocity, adjusted payments, Visa and Mastercard settlement and bank entries are the "real economy" signals bulls wanted, but they do not yet move supply. This means a disconnect has opened between the "usage narrative" and the size of the "float". Investors and analysts relying on supply growth (issuer revenues, T-bill demand, Circle valuation) face a headwind until the float grows again.
- **Leading indicators to watch:**
  - The OCC and other GENIUS final rules (31 Oct target), then the GENIUS effective date (18 Jan 2027 at the latest).
  - Open USD's supply ramp (about $0.83B in three weeks).
  - The 21-bank coin (H1 2027), Qivalis (H2 2026) and HSBC RedCoin.
  - CLARITY after the midterms.
  - CEX stablecoin balances turning up from about $64B.
  - Recovery in total crypto market cap and perp funding, which would revive USDe/USDS-type float.
  - The Fed path after the 16 Sep hike.
- **Net classification for a later "trend momentum" judgment:** "**fundamentally intact but decelerating in 2026 on supply; accelerating on adoption infrastructure.**" The tailwinds are structural (regulation, distribution, rails). The headwinds are cyclical (crypto bear market, yield compression in DeFi, a hawkish Fed) and competitive (fragmentation of the yield pie away from Tether and Circle).
